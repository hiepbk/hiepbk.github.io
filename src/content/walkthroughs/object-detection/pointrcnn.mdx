## Motivation & Main Contribution

- So far the series has pushed points into grids: BEV images (**MV3D**), voxels (**VoxelNet**, **SECOND**),
  pillars (**PointPillars**). Every grid quantises the scan and loses some detail.
- PointRCNN goes back to the **PointNet** road: no voxels, no BEV image, no camera — raw points end to end.
- It is a **two-stage, point-only** detector in the spirit of Faster R-CNN:
  1. **Stage 1 — bottom-up proposals.** Segment foreground points and predict a 3D box from each one.
  2. **Stage 2 — canonical refinement.** Pool the points inside each proposal, rotate and shift them into
     the proposal's own frame, and refine the box and its confidence.
- **Main contributions:** proposals from segmented points instead of anchors; refinement in canonical
  coordinates; a **bin-based** box loss (classify a bin, then regress inside it).
- At submission it ranked first among published methods on the KITTI 3D car test board, using LiDAR only.

## Challenges at the Time

- **Anchors in 3D are expensive.** AVOD places 80–100k anchor boxes in 3D space and pools multi-view
  features for each one.
- **Image-first pipelines miss objects.** F-PointNet crops a frustum from a 2D detection; an object the
  camera misses (occlusion, darkness) can never be recovered in 3D.
- **Quantisation.** BEV projection and voxelization lose information, and dense 3D CNNs are slow.
- **Large search space.** A 3D box has 7 parameters, including a heading angle; direct regression of
  all of them from scratch is hard to train.

## Naive Questions

- **Where do the segmentation labels come from?**
  For free: in driving scenes objects do not overlap, so every point inside a ground-truth box is foreground.
- **Why predict a box from *every* foreground point?**
  Each point sees its object through the backbone's receptive field; NMS then keeps a few good proposals.
- **Why not regress the centre offset directly?**
  The paper finds that classifying a bin and regressing a small residual is more accurate and converges faster.
- **Why rotate the pooled points into the proposal's frame?**
  Then every car looks "the same way round", and stage 2 only has to learn small corrections.
- **Doesn't the canonical frame throw away distance?**
  Yes — so the distance to the sensor is added back as an extra feature.
- **Is PointNet++ fast enough for a full scan?**
  It runs on 16,384 sampled points; sampling and grouping cost is the price of staying point-based.

## Key Idea

> **Key idea.** In 3D the boxes already give a segmentation mask for free — so segment the foreground
> points first, grow a proposal from each of them, and refine each proposal in its own coordinate frame.

Bin-based localisation for a foreground point $p$ at $(x^{(p)}, z^{(p)})$ whose object centre is $(x^p, z^p)$:

$$
\mathrm{bin}_u^{(p)} = \left\lfloor \frac{u^p - u^{(p)} + S}{\delta} \right\rfloor, \qquad
\mathrm{res}_u^{(p)} = \frac{1}{C}\left(u^p - u^{(p)} + S - \left(\mathrm{bin}_u^{(p)} \cdot \delta + \frac{\delta}{2}\right)\right), \quad u \in \{x, z\}
$$

- $S$: search range around the point along each ground-plane axis; $\delta$: bin length; $C$: bin length for normalisation.
- The bin is trained with cross-entropy, the residual with smooth L1. The heading $\theta$ is handled the same
  way with $n$ angle bins; the height $y$ and size $(h, w, l)$ are plain residual regression (size relative to
  the class-average size).

### The pipeline, step by step

1. **Input.** Sample 16,384 points per scene (repeat points if a scene has fewer).
2. **Backbone.** PointNet++ with multi-scale grouping: four set-abstraction levels, then four
   feature-propagation levels back to every point (the encoder–decoder from the PointNet walkthrough).

> **Builds on.** PointNet++ with multi-scale grouping as the backbone
> ([walkthrough 01](/walkthroughs/object-detection/pointnet/)), and the two-stage recipe of R-CNN / Faster R-CNN from 2D detection.
3. **Stage-1 heads.** Per point: a foreground score (focal loss) and a bin-based box (only foreground points
   are supervised).
4. **Proposal NMS.** Oriented BEV IoU NMS. Training: threshold 0.85, keep top 300. Inference: threshold 0.8,
   keep top 100.
5. **Point cloud region pooling.** Enlarge each proposal by $\eta$ and keep the points inside, with their
   stage-1 features.
6. **Canonical transformation** of the pooled points, then a small PointNet++ encoder.
7. **Stage-2 heads.** Confidence (cross-entropy) and bin-based box refinement; final oriented NMS with
   BEV IoU 0.01.

### Tensor shapes

| Stage | Operation → purpose | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Input | raw scene points, randomly sampled → *a fixed number of points per scene* | $16384$ points |
| SA 1–4 (MSG) | sample and group at several radii → *fewer points, each with wider context* | $4096 \to 1024 \to 256 \to 64$ points |
| FP 1–4 | interpolate back up → *a rich feature for every input point* | $16384 \times C$ (per-point features) |
| Stage-1 heads | foreground mask + bin-based box → *every foreground point proposes a box, no anchors* | one score and one box per point |
| NMS | oriented BEV NMS → *a few good, non-overlapping proposals* | 300 (train) / 100 (test) proposals |
| Region pooling | points in the enlarged box, random sample → *the points (and features) that describe each proposal, plus a little context* | $512$ points per proposal |
| Canonical transform | rotate + translate into the box frame, add depth → *the same car looks the same wherever it is; depth keeps the distance cue* | $512$ points per proposal |
| Stage-2 SA (SSG) | sample and group → *encode each proposal's points into one vector* | $128 \to 32 \to 1$ |
| Stage-2 heads | confidence + box refinement → *score and correct each proposal* | one score and one box per proposal |

- The paper does not state the backbone channel width $C$, so it stays a symbol here.

### Stage 1: segmentation as the proposal generator

- Foreground points are a small minority in an outdoor scan → **focal loss**
  $L_{focal}(p_t) = -\alpha_t (1 - p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)$ with $\alpha_t = 0.25$, $\gamma = 2$.
- Ground-truth boxes are enlarged by 0.2 m on each side, and background points in that margin are ignored,
  because box annotations are not pixel-perfect.
- Hyper-parameters (car): $S = 3$ m, $\delta = 0.5$ m, $n = 12$ heading bins.
- Why it is cheap: no anchors at all — the number of candidates is the number of foreground points.

> **Builds on.** Focal loss (RetinaNet) for the foreground mask, and F-PointNet's bin-based heading,
> extended here to the centre location.

### Point cloud region pooling: keep a bit of context

- Each proposal $b_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i, h_i, w_i, l_i, \theta_i)$ is enlarged to
  $(x_i, y_i, z_i, h_i + \eta, w_i + \eta, l_i + \eta, \theta_i)$.
- Every point inside keeps: coordinates, intensity $r^{(p)}$, predicted mask $m^{(p)} \in \{0, 1\}$, and the
  stage-1 feature $f^{(p)} \in \mathbb{R}^C$. Proposals with no points are dropped.
- $\eta = 1.0$ m works best (Table 5): no context gives 75.68 moderate AP, $\eta = 1.0$ m gives 77.67;
  larger margins start to swallow points of neighbouring objects.

### Canonical transformation: refine in the box's own frame

- Each proposal gets its own coordinate system: origin at the box centre, $X'$ along the heading,
  $Z'$ perpendicular to it on the ground plane, $Y'$ unchanged.
- Pooled points are rotated and translated into this frame; the proposal becomes $(0, 0, 0, h, w, l, 0)$ and
  stage 2 learns only the residual to the ground truth.
- The frame removes distance information, so the depth $d^{(p)} = \sqrt{(x^{(p)})^2 + (y^{(p)})^2 + (z^{(p)})^2}$ is
  added back. The local inputs $[\tilde p, r^{(p)}, m^{(p)}, d^{(p)}]$ go through FC layers, are concatenated with
  $f^{(p)}$, and feed the stage-2 PointNet++ encoder.
- This is the single most important step: without it, moderate AP drops from 77.67 to **13.68** (Table 4).
  Removing the stage-1 features $f^{(p)}$ costs 2.71 moderate AP.

### Stage 2: bins again, but smaller

- Targets: positive for classification if max 3D IoU > 0.6, negative if < 0.45; box regression on proposals
  with 3D IoU ≥ 0.55.
- Smaller search range $S = 1.5$ m, $\delta = 0.5$ m. Since IoU ≥ 0.55, the heading error is assumed to lie in
  $[-\pi/4, \pi/4]$ and is split into bins of $\omega = 10°$.
- Loss: cross-entropy on confidence over all sampled proposals + bin and residual losses over the positive ones.
- The two stages are trained **separately**: stage 1 for 200 epochs (batch 16), stage 2 for 50 epochs (batch 256), both at lr 0.002.
- Augmentation includes **GT-AUG** — pasting ground-truth boxes and their points from other scenes, the
  sampling trick from SECOND.

> **Builds on.** SECOND's ground-truth sampling augmentation
> ([walkthrough 04](/walkthroughs/object-detection/second/)).

### Pros and Cons

- **Pros**
  - No anchors and no grid: proposals grow from segmented points, with high recall from few proposals.
  - Segmentation labels come for free from the boxes.
  - Canonical refinement makes stage 2 learn small residuals — removing it costs most of the accuracy (Table 4).
  - LiDAR only, yet ahead of the camera + LiDAR methods on cars at the time.
- **Cons**
  - A PointNet++ backbone over the whole scan is costly (sampling and grouping 16,384 points).
  - The two stages are trained separately.
  - Pedestrians lag behind camera + LiDAR methods — few points on small objects.

### Future Work

- Keep point-level refinement, but get proposals and scene features from a faster backbone.
- Train both stages end to end.
