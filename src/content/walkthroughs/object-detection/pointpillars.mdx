## Motivation & Main Contribution

- **VoxelNet** runs at 4.4 Hz; **SECOND** reaches 20 Hz with sparse convolution, but its 3D layers remain the bottleneck.
- Question: do we need 3D convolution at all? After the middle layers, both detectors flatten height into channels anyway.
- PointPillars answers no: use **pillars** — voxels spanning the full height — so the grid is 2D from the start.
- **Main contributions:**
  - A **pillar encoder**: a simplified PointNet per pillar, scattered into a 2D **pseudo-image**.
  - A detector built only from **2D convolutions** that runs at **62 Hz**, 2–4× faster than prior methods.
  - State of the art on KITTI BEV and 3D among published methods by mAP, LiDAR only.
- Reading tip: the pillar encoder is the simplest possible use of **PointNet** in a detector — one linear layer and a max.

## Challenges at the Time

- **3D convolutions are slow**, even sparse ones: VoxelNet's encoder takes 190 ms; SECOND's open-source encoder 48 ms.
- **Fixed BEV encoders** (MV3D, PIXOR, Complex-YOLO) are fast but hand-crafted and need re-engineering for new sensors.
- **Vertical binning** is one more hyper-parameter to tune by hand.
- **Sparsity:** at $0.16^2$ m² cells a 64-beam scan has only 6k–9k non-empty pillars in the KITTI range — about 97% empty.

## Naive Questions

- **Doesn't a pillar lose all height information?**
  No: each point keeps its $z$ (and offsets) as input features; the PointNet learns the vertical shape.
- **Then why did VoxelNet need 3D convolutions?**
  To aggregate across height; with pillars that aggregation happens inside the per-pillar PointNet.
- **How can a PointNet output become an image?**
  Each pillar vector is written back to its $(x, y)$ cell; empty cells stay zero → a $C \times H \times W$ pseudo-image.
- **Isn't one linear layer too weak?**
  The paper uses one PointNet instead of VoxelNet's two; it saved 2.5 ms with no loss in accuracy.
- **Why care about 62 Hz if a LiDAR spins at 20 Hz?**
  KITTI uses only the ~10% of points in the camera view, and cars run on embedded GPUs.
- **Learned vs fixed encoder — how big is the gap?**
  At equal backbone, learned encoders win at every resolution, and more so with larger pillars (Table 4).

## Key Idea

> **Key idea.** Make each voxel a full-height pillar, encode it with a tiny PointNet, scatter the vectors into a
> 2D pseudo-image, and detect with a 2D CNN — no 3D convolution anywhere.

Encoder for pillar $p$ with points $\hat l_1, \dots, \hat l_N$:

$$
f_p = \operatorname{MAX}_{i=1..N}\, \operatorname{ReLU}\big(\operatorname{BN}(W \hat l_i)\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{C},
\qquad \text{pseudo-image}[:, y_p, x_p] = f_p
$$

- $\hat l_i \in \mathbb{R}^9$: the decorated point $(x, y, z, r, x_c, y_c, z_c, x_p, y_p)$ — see below.
- $W$: one linear layer (a $1 \times 1$ convolution), shared by all points and pillars; $C = 64$.
- $(x_p, y_p)$ in the second formula: the pillar's grid cell.

### PointPillars, step by step (car network)

Range $x \in (0, 70.4)$, $y \in (-40, 40)$, $z \in (-3, 1)$ m; pillar size 0.16 m; at most $P = 12000$ pillars and
$N = 100$ points per pillar (Section 4.2):

| Stage | Operation | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Input | points inside the camera view | $n \times 4$ $(x, y, z, r)$ |
| Pillarize + decorate | group by $(x, y)$ cell, add 5 features, sample or zero-pad | $D \times P \times N = 9 \times 12000 \times 100$ |
| Linear + BN + ReLU | shared over all points | $64 \times 12000 \times 100$ |
| Max | over the points of each pillar | $64 \times 12000$ |
| Scatter | write each pillar to its cell | $64 \times H \times W$ |
| Block1(S=2, L=4, F=64) | 3×3 convs | $64 \times H/2 \times W/2$ |
| Block2(4, 6, 128) | | $128 \times H/4 \times W/4$ |
| Block3(8, 6, 256) | | $256 \times H/8 \times W/8$ |
| Up1, Up2, Up3 → stride 2 | transposed conv to 128 each, concat | $384 \times H/2 \times W/2$ |
| SSD head | anchors at $0°$ / $90°$ | class, box (7), direction |

- From the stated range and pillar size, $H \times W = 500 \times 440$ cells.
- Pedestrian / cyclist: first block stride $S = 1$ instead of 2; range $x \in (0, 48)$, $y \in (-20, 20)$, $z \in (-2.5, 0.5)$ m.

### Pillars and point decoration

- Grid only in $x$–$y$; **no binning in $z$**.
- Each point $(x, y, z, r)$ gets:
  - $x_c, y_c, z_c$: offset from the mean of the points in its pillar (VoxelNet's decoration);
  - $x_p, y_p$: offset from the pillar's $x, y$ centre (new; +0.5 mAP and more reproducible runs, Section 7.3).

- Fixed-size tensor $(D, P, N)$: too many pillars or points → random sampling; too few → zero padding.

> **Builds on.** VoxelNet's decoration (offsets to the mean of the points, [walkthrough 03](/walkthroughs/object-detection/voxelnet/)); $x_p, y_p$ are new.

### Pillar Feature Net: PointNet with one layer

- Linear → BatchNorm → ReLU on every point, then max over the pillar's points: exactly PointNet's
  "shared MLP + max pool", with an MLP of depth one.
- Compared with **VoxelNet's VFE**: one layer instead of two stacked VFEs, and no concatenation back to the points.
- Encoding time **1.3 ms** vs 190 ms for VoxelNet's encoder (Section 6).

> **Builds on.** PointNet's shared MLP + max pool ([walkthrough 01](/walkthroughs/object-detection/pointnet/)).

### Backbone and head: just a 2D detector

- Backbone like VoxelNet's RPN: a top-down path of three blocks, each block upsampled back to stride $S$ and concatenated.
- **SSD** head; anchors matched by 2D (BEV) IoU, height and elevation are extra regression targets.
- Car anchor $w, l, h = (1.6, 3.9, 1.5)$ m at $z = -1$ m, thresholds 0.6 / 0.45.
  Pedestrian $(0.6, 0.8, 1.73)$ and cyclist $(0.6, 1.76, 1.73)$ at $z = -0.6$ m, thresholds 0.5 / 0.35.
- Axis-aligned NMS at IoU 0.5: similar accuracy to rotated NMS, much faster.

> **Builds on.** VoxelNet's RPN-style backbone and anchors, and the SSD detection head.

### Loss: SECOND's recipe

$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{N_{pos}} \left( \beta_{loc} \mathcal{L}_{loc} + \beta_{cls} \mathcal{L}_{cls} + \beta_{dir} \mathcal{L}_{dir} \right),
\qquad \beta_{loc} = 2,\ \beta_{cls} = 1,\ \beta_{dir} = 0.2
$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{loc}$: Smooth $\ell_1$ on the 7 residuals, with $\Delta\theta = \sin(\theta^{gt} - \theta^a)$ (SECOND).
- $\mathcal{L}_{cls}$: focal loss, $\alpha = 0.25$, $\gamma = 2$. $\mathcal{L}_{dir}$: softmax over discretised headings.
- Adam, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$, decay 0.8 every 15 epochs, 160 epochs; weights initialised randomly (no pre-training).

> **Builds on.** SECOND's losses (sine angle residual, focal loss, direction classifier) and GT-sampling ([walkthrough 04](/walkthroughs/object-detection/second/)).

### Where the speed comes from

- Runtime breakdown (car network, i7 CPU + 1080 Ti GPU): load and filter 1.4 ms, pillarize and decorate 2.7 ms,
  upload to GPU 2.9 ms, encode 1.3 ms, scatter 0.1 ms, backbone + head 7.7 ms, NMS 0.1 ms → **16.2 ms**.
- **Slimmer design:** one PointNet (−2.5 ms), first block lowered to 64 channels (−4.5 ms), upsampled features
  halved to 128 (−3.9 ms) — none of them hurt accuracy.
- **TensorRT:** 45.5% faster than the 42.4 Hz PyTorch pipeline.
- **Pillar size trades speed for accuracy:** sizes from $0.12^2$ to $0.28^2$ m²; at $0.28^2$ m² it runs at **105 Hz**
  at similar accuracy to previous methods (Fig. 5).

### Augmentation

- **GT-sampling** from SECOND: 15 cars, 0 pedestrians, 8 cyclists pasted per sample.
- **Per-box:** rotation $U[-\pi/20, \pi/20]$, translation $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.25)$ — much milder than VoxelNet / SECOND;
  more per-box augmentation hurt pedestrians (Section 7.2).
- **Global:** mirror flip along $x$, rotation and scaling, translation $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.2)$.

## Conclusion

### Results

KITTI test, 3D detection (Table 2 of the paper; mAP over car / pedestrian / cyclist, moderate):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MV3D | LiDAR & image | 2.8 | N/A | 71.09 | 62.35 | 55.12 |
| ContFuse | LiDAR & image | 16.7 | N/A | 82.54 | 66.22 | 64.04 |
| RoarNet | LiDAR & image | 10 | N/A | **83.71** | 73.04 | 59.16 |
| AVOD-FPN | LiDAR & image | 10 | 55.62 | 81.94 | 71.88 | 66.38 |
| F-PointNet | LiDAR & image | 5.9 | 57.35 | 81.20 | 70.39 | 62.19 |
| VoxelNet | LiDAR | 4.4 | 49.05 | 77.47 | 65.11 | 57.73 |
| SECOND | LiDAR | 20 | 56.69 | 83.13 | 73.66 | 66.20 |

- **BEV** (Table 1): car 88.35 / 86.10 / 79.83; mAP (moderate) 66.19 — the highest in the table.
- **Validation, moderate:** 3D AP 77.98 (car), 57.86 (pedestrian), 66.02 (cyclist).
- **Encoder study** (Table 4, BEV mAP on val, same network): at $0.16^2$ m² PointPillars 73.7 vs VoxelNet 74.4 vs
  MV3D's fixed encoder 72.8; at $0.28^2$ m² 72.0 vs 71.9 vs 67.6.

### Pros and Cons

- **Pros**
  - No 3D convolution anywhere: a one-layer PointNet per pillar, then a plain 2D CNN — 62 Hz (105 Hz with larger pillars).
  - Learned encoder: no hand-tuned vertical binning, and it beats fixed encoders at every pillar size.
  - Simple to implement and deploy (TensorRT); it became the go-to fast LiDAR baseline.
- **Cons**
  - Not the best everywhere: lower easy-car 3D AP than SECOND.
  - Pedestrians and cyclists are confused with each other and with poles.
  - One vector summarises a whole pillar, so fine vertical detail is compressed early.

### Future Work

- Recover fine geometry from the raw points, e.g. a second refinement stage.
- Better small-object (pedestrian, cyclist) detection.
