## Motivation & Main Contribution

- Every detector so far in the series — VoxelNet, SECOND, PointPillars, PV-RCNN — places **anchor boxes**
  on the BEV map. PointRCNN avoided anchors, but only with a costly point-based backbone.
- CenterPoint keeps the standard backbones (**VoxelNet/SECOND-style sparse voxels** or **PointPillars**) and
  changes only the **output representation**: an object is a **point** — its centre in BEV.
- It brings the 2D detector **CenterNet** (objects as keypoints) to LiDAR.
- **Main contributions:**
  1. A **centre heatmap head** plus dense regression heads for size, height, heading and velocity.
  2. A light **second stage** that reads features at five points on the box (centre and four side faces).
  3. **Tracking by closest-point matching**, using a predicted velocity — no Kalman filter.
- The switch from anchors to centres alone gives about +4 mAPH on Waymo for both backbones.

## Challenges at the Time

- **3D boxes are rotated; anchors are not.** Anchors come at fixed headings (e.g. 0° and 90°); a car turning
  at 45° fits none of them well.
- **More anchors are not a fix.** One template per heading adds compute and false positives.
- **Target assignment is fiddly.** Anchor detectors use BEV IoU thresholds per class and per dataset
  (e.g. 0.55 / 0.4 for vehicles, 0.5 / 0.35 for pedestrians on Waymo, following PV-RCNN).
- **Very different shapes.** Bicycles are nearly flat, buses long, pedestrians tall — a few anchor sizes
  cover them poorly.
- **Two-stage refinement is slow.** PointNet-style RoI pooling (PointRCNN, PV-RCNN) costs a lot of time.

## Naive Questions

- **Why is a point better than a box?**
  A point has no orientation, so there is nothing to align; heading becomes just another regressed value.
- **How do you get a box from a point?**
  Read size, height and heading from the regression maps at the peak's location.
- **Do we still need NMS?**
  Each local maximum is one object; the paper still applies NMS before the second stage.
- **Why make the Gaussian bigger than in CenterNet?**
  Objects in BEV are small and sparse, so a tiny peak gives almost no positive supervision.
- **How can tracking be a nearest-neighbour search?**
  The network predicts each object's velocity; move the current centres back one frame and match the closest track.
- **Why a second stage if the first works?**
  The centre feature may not see the object: LiDAR often only hits one side of a car.

## Key Idea

> **Key idea.** Represent each object by its centre in BEV: find centres as heatmap peaks, regress all
> other box properties at the peak, and track by matching centres.

- **Heatmap target.** For $K$ classes, a $K$-channel heatmap $\hat Y$; each ground-truth centre is rendered as a
  2D Gaussian with radius

$$
\sigma = \max\big(f(wl),\, \tau\big), \qquad \tau = 2
$$

- $w, l$: box width and length; $f$: CornerNet's radius function; $\tau$: smallest allowed radius. Trained with focal loss.
- **Final score** after the second stage — the geometric mean of the two stages:

$$
\hat Q_t = \sqrt{\hat Y_t \cdot \hat I_t}
$$

- $\hat Y_t$: first-stage heatmap score of object $t$; $\hat I_t$: second-stage IoU-guided confidence.

### The pipeline, step by step

1. **3D backbone** (unchanged from prior work): VoxelNet-style sparse voxels or PointPillars → map-view
   features $M \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times L \times F}$.
2. **First stage, dense heads**: a shared $3 \times 3$ conv + BN + ReLU, then one branch per output
   (two $3 \times 3$ convs with BN + ReLU in between).
3. **Decode**: take heatmap peaks; read the regression maps at each peak.
4. **Second stage**: bilinear-sample $M$ at five points per box, concatenate, MLP → IoU-guided score and box refinement.
5. **Tracking**: velocity-based greedy closest-point matching.

> **Builds on.** CenterNet's keypoint heatmap (objects as points) from 2D detection; the backbones of
> VoxelNet / SECOND and PointPillars ([walkthroughs 03](/walkthroughs/object-detection/voxelnet/),
> [04](/walkthroughs/object-detection/second/), [05](/walkthroughs/object-detection/pointpillars/)).

### Tensor shapes

The pipeline, stage by stage:

| Stage | Operation | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Input | LiDAR points (current and previous sweep) | raw points |
| 3D backbone | VoxelNet-style sparse voxels or PointPillars | map view $M$: $W \times L \times F$ |
| Shared conv | $3 \times 3$ conv + BN + ReLU | $W \times L \times C$ |
| Heatmap head | two $3 \times 3$ convs | $W \times L \times K$ |
| Regression heads | two $3 \times 3$ convs per output | $W \times L \times 10$ (2 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 2) |
| Peak decode | local maxima, read the maps at each peak | one box per peak |
| Second stage | 5 bilinear features → MLP | score + refined box per box |
| Tracking | back-project by $-v$, greedy closest match | tracks |

The first-stage outputs at each BEV cell:

| Output | Meaning | Channels | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heatmap $\hat Y$ | centre likelihood per class | $K$ | focal |
| Offset $o$ | sub-voxel refinement of the centre | 2 | L1 |
| Height $h_g$ | height above ground | 1 | L1 |
| Size $s$ | 3D size, regressed in log space | 3 | L1 |
| Heading | $(\sin\alpha, \cos\alpha)$ | 2 | L1 |
| Velocity $v$ | BEV displacement to the previous frame | 2 | L1 |

- All maps are $W \times L$, the size of $M$. Regression is supervised only at ground-truth centres.
- Input settings — **Waymo**: range $[-75.2, 75.2]$ m in X and Y, $[-2, 4]$ m in Z; voxels $(0.1, 0.1, 0.15)$ m
  (as in PV-RCNN), pillars $0.32 \times 0.32$ m. **nuScenes**: $[-51.2, 51.2]$ m, $[-5, 3]$ m; voxels
  $(0.1, 0.1, 0.2)$ m, pillars $0.2 \times 0.2$ m.

### The centre heatmap: why it beats anchors

- No orientation to match → the backbone can learn rotation invariance; heading is regressed, not enumerated.
- No IoU thresholds for assignment: one positive cell per object, at its centre.
- Gains are largest where anchors struggle (Waymo val, LEVEL 2 mAPH):
  - objects rotated 30°–45°: vehicles 45.4 → **51.6**, pedestrians 26.5 → **35.7** (Table 7);
  - small pedestrians: 29.6 → **38.5** (Table 8).
- nuScenes test shows the same pattern: the largest gains are on extreme shapes (+6.4 mAP bicycle, +7.0 construction vehicle).

### Two-stage CenterPoint: five points, not a RoI

- The box centre and the top and bottom face centres project to the same BEV point, so the second stage uses
  the **centre + the four side-face centres** — five features from $M$ by bilinear interpolation.
- Head: shared two-layer MLP (BN, ReLU, dropout 0.3), then two branches of three FC layers — score and box refinement.
- Score target as in PV-RCNN: $I = \min(1, \max(0, 2\,\mathrm{IoU}_t - 0.5))$, binary cross-entropy;
  box refinement with L1.

> **Builds on.** PV-RCNN's IoU-guided score target and its Waymo settings
> ([walkthrough 07](/walkthroughs/object-detection/pv-rcnn/)).
- Training: 128 boxes per scene, 1:1 positive/negative, positive if IoU ≥ 0.55; inference on the top 500 after NMS.
- Cost (Waymo val, VoxelNet backbone): first stage 71 ms; the surface-centre second stage adds **6 ms** and lifts
  LEVEL 2 mAPH from 66.5 / 62.7 to 68.3 / 65.3 (vehicle / pedestrian, Table 9).
- BEV features are enough: adding PV-RCNN's VSA voxel features keeps 68.3 / 65.2 but raises run time
  from 77 ms to 98 ms (Table 10).

### Velocity head and tracking

- The velocity head needs two map-views (current and previous frame) and predicts the centre's displacement.
- Tracking: move each current centre back by $-v$, then **greedily** match it to the closest track
  (class-wise distance threshold); unmatched tracks survive up to $T = 3$ frames.
- No hidden state, no motion model: tracking takes **1 ms**, vs. 73 ms for a Mahalanobis Kalman-filter tracker
  (Table 12, nuScenes val).

> **Builds on.** CenterTrack's offset-based tracking from 2D, and SORT's rule for keeping unmatched tracks.

### Pros and Cons

- **Pros**
  - A point has no orientation: rotated objects and odd shapes stop being special cases; no anchor tuning.
  - Works on top of standard backbones (VoxelNet, PointPillars) — only the head changes.
  - Second stage for 6 ms; tracking for 1 ms, with no Kalman filter.
- **Cons**
  - The second stage reads only the 2D BEV map — no help on nuScenes' sparse 32-beam LiDAR.
  - Small objects can occupy a single BEV cell (PointPillars pedestrians).
  - Velocity needs two frames as input.

### Future Work

- Give refinement real 3D context without the cost of point-based RoI pooling.
- Better features for sparse and small objects.
